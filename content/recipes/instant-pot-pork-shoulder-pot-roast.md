## Ingredients

- 1.53 lb pork shoulder (butt) steak, cut into 2-3 large chunks
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium onion, sliced
- 2 carrots, cut into 1-inch pieces
- 2 celery stalks, cut into 1-inch pieces
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed
- 2 tbsp Shaoxing wine (for deglazing)
- 1½ cups beef broth (Better Than Bouillon)
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tbsp water (slurry)
- 1 tsp apple cider vinegar (to finish)
- Salt and black pepper, to taste

## Process

1. Season pork chunks generously with salt and pepper. Sear on all sides in olive oil using Instant Pot's sauté function, in batches if needed, until deeply browned — this is where most of the flavor comes from, don't rush it. Remove and set aside.
1. In the same pot, sauté onion, carrot, and celery until the onion softens and starts to pick up color, about 4-5 minutes. Add garlic, cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
1. Deglaze with Shaoxing wine, scraping up all the browned bits.
1. Return pork to the pot. Add beef broth, soy sauce, and Worcestershire — these two together do the work tomato paste would normally do, adding savory depth without breaking the nightshade rule. Add bay leaf.
1. Pressure cook on high for 40 minutes, then let pressure release naturally for 15 minutes before venting the rest.
1. Remove pork and vegetables. Switch pot back to sauté, bring liquid to a simmer, and stir in cornstarch slurry to thicken into a light gravy, 1-2 minutes.
1. Off heat, stir in apple cider vinegar — a small hit of acid at the end wakes the whole dish up. Taste and adjust salt.
1. Return pork and vegetables to the pot to coat in gravy, or plate separately with gravy spooned over.

**Notes:** The vinegar at the end is doing what tomato acidity usually would in a pot roast — don't skip it. If you want it darker/richer, an extra teaspoon of Worcestershire won't overpower.
